# Rust Raft ledger — project record and future direction

The follow-on [candidate qualification](release-qualification.md) records the fixes, local evidence and production decision following this assessment.

**9 November 2026.** The seven-crate structure is a sound foundation for backend integration. The next milestone is to demonstrate what each deployment can recover, how quickly payments resume, and how an operator verifies that three voting disks are healthy again. Production latency targets and the acceptable disaster-loss window are still product decisions.

## What a successful payment means

The ledger orders commands for one book and asset. It uses integer amounts, balanced multi-leg postings, account limits, ordered funding sources, reservations, partial captures and explicit release of the remainder. A first request ID records the command and its accepted or rejected decision. A matching retry returns that decision. Reuse with different fields returns an ID conflict. A timeout leaves the result uncertain, so a caller keeps the original ID and payload. [Financial contracts and design index](../ARCHITECTURE.md) · [Client protocol](rfcs/0009-client-protocol.md)

| Deployment | When the client receives a decision | Recovery consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three voters with a synchronous recovery journal | A voting majority commits, the leader applies, and the independent journal confirms coverage. A current read also needs a quorum read barrier and fresh journal coverage of the sampled state. | Protected replies pause during journal outages. A surviving, independently durable journal can reproduce every protected acknowledgement, including rejections. |
| Three voters plus one permanent, non-voting learner | A voting majority commits and the leader applies. Current reads use a quorum barrier and local application. Voters can serve while the learner, archive or broker is offline. | After total replica loss, recovery reaches the verified archive cut. Later acknowledged payments can be lost. With archive export disabled, this mode supplies no independent recovery copy. |

Membership is fixed for a generation. Two voters form a majority. One voter and the learner do not. A journal-free three-voter node also has an embedded diagnostic interface whose local view can lag. Migration to learner mode requires a new generation and isolation of the old voting majority. A journal fence alone cannot stop voters that acknowledge independently of the journal. The directory layout gives a new contributor several entry points. The [workspace manifest](../Cargo.toml) lists all seven crates. Inside each crate, `src/lib.rs` exposes its public interface. The node’s [binary entry point](../crates/ledger-node/src/main.rs) starts the process and dispatches operator commands. Cross-process scenarios live in [node integration tests](../crates/ledger-node/tests), and the [architecture index](../ARCHITECTURE.md) links the design decisions to their implementations. The accounting rules can be tested without running Raft or RocksDB. The public API depends on those accounting types and can be built independently of the server. **Three paths to know when making a change**

1. **Payment.** The gRPC handler normalizes the request. The leader batches queued commands, persists Raft state, and applies committed commands in order. One projection write publishes changed accounts, reservations, the decision, its indexes and the applied position. The configured service mode determines when the reply can leave. [Public handler](../crates/ledger-node/src/client_service.rs) · [Raft owner](../crates/ledger-node/src/runtime.rs) · [Persistence and apply](../crates/ledger-replica/src/replica.rs) · [Projection write](../crates/ledger-replica/src/projection/delta.rs)
2. **Restart and catch-up.** The working projection has its own WAL disabled. A voter rebuilds it from its sealed checkpoint and durable committed tail, or from genesis before its first checkpoint. A returning member can receive ordinary appends, a physical snapshot, or verified replay from an eligible local seal. Snapshot transport stages data before Raft adopts it. Receipts and journal entries remain indexed. [Snapshot protocol](rfcs/0012-incremental-snapshots.md) · [Transport](../crates/ledger-node/src/transport/snapshots/receive.rs) · [Snapshot installation](../crates/ledger-replica/src/replica/snapshot.rs)
3. **Learner export.** The archive receives original committed Raft bytes. Kafka receives one logical event per first business decision, including rejections. When both destinations are enabled, publishing waits for confirmed archive coverage. With Kafka alone it follows the learner’s applied position. Delivery is at least once. Consumers should commit the stable ledger/request ID alongside their own side effect. In Kafka-only mode, downstream consumers may have acted on decisions beyond a later disaster restore point. Reconcile those events against the recovered ledger before resuming side effects. Kafka records cannot reconstruct the consensus history. [Archive contract](rfcs/0019-async-recovery-archive.md) · [Event contract](rfcs/0020-ledger-decision-publishing.md)

The Raft owner collects up to 256 queued commands or 1 MiB per entry. It allows up to eight proposals in flight, with admission capped at 512 requests and 8 MiB. The byte-bounded account cache improved local reads on a hot working set. A wider traversal evicted an account on every read in the recorded benchmark. Receipts and postings remain indexed as history grows. [Batching and group commit](rfcs/0011-microbatching-and-group-commit.md) · [Cache measurements](rfcs/0010-account-cache.md) · [Ledger history](rfcs/0014-immutable-ledger-history.md)

The crate structure is professional-grade for backend integration. Keep the seven crates. Within the node, separate client-handle and admission logic from process startup as those parts evolve. Today they share the same host module. The most valuable code cleanup is to give the service mode one authoritative representation shared by startup, read admission and reply handling. The README now lists all seven crates and the architecture diagram shows both topologies. Kafka is optional at runtime. The ordinary node build still includes the Kafka adapter and native TLS libraries. ## Remaining engineering risks

- **Loss of a voting disk.** A member with an intact disk can catch up. The supplied fixed-membership workflows cannot return a permanently lost voter to service online from a blank disk in the same generation. Two surviving voters have no further voting failure tolerance. A permanent learner adds a data copy and cannot be promoted by editing its config. [Membership](rfcs/0018-static-raft-learner.md) · [Recovery runbook](../README.md)
- **Catch-up under heavy load.** The five-crate source review identified Raft append batching that could outgrow the 4 MiB transport limit and a synchronous checkpoint flush on the sole Raft owner that could delay heartbeats. Physical snapshots fetched and synced serial 256 KiB chunks under a 24-hour offer. Large or superseded offers could lose progress across leadership or checkpoint changes. A slow-link drill with a large backlog would measure the impact. [Assessment and source pointers](../output/pdf/ledger-engineering-assessment.pdf)
- **Recovery maintenance.** Full journal replay can restore a voter when a published recovery base is damaged. That fallback leaves the damaged base in place. Interrupted restores can leave full staging directories, and physically reclaimed voter history depends on the pinned independent image. Learner-mode voters retain raw local history, so storage growth also needs measurement. [Recovery bases](rfcs/0016-journal-checkpoints.md) · [Reclamation](rfcs/0017-raft-log-reclamation.md) · [Learner archive](rfcs/0019-async-recovery-archive.md)
- **Operator and client workflows.** Live voter progress is available to embedded code, and the learner logs export progress. The review found no standalone authenticated voter catch-up command, public lookup for a partially captured hold’s remaining lines, or structured distinction between temporary saturation and permanently oversized requests. The protected setup example required creating the journal’s parent directory, and the public example started with a lookup. The dedicated backend CA also grants every trusted backend access to every mutation, including changing bounds. [Node status](../crates/ledger-node/src/host.rs) · [Public schema](../crates/ledger-api/proto/ledger/v1/ledger.proto) · [Setup and trust model](../README.md)

## Proposed future direction

| Order | Focus | Evidence that would close it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Choose the acknowledgement contract for the first deployment.** Agree on the tolerable loss window, required availability during a backup outage, and recovery times. Make the active mode visible. | A backend owner and an operator can each explain what a successful payment guarantees if every replica disk is lost one second later. For learner mode, define the archive lag that triggers an alert and the response when it exceeds that threshold. |
| 2 | **Make recovery observable and rehearse it.** Expose authenticated role, term, peer progress, applied index, verified archive/journal cut, broker cursor and recent errors. Record journal-lock wait and hold times. | On separate hosts with independently durable storage, exercise leader loss, a blank voter disk, and an archive outage during writes. Reconcile original receipts and holds. Record time to first payment, time to three healthy voters, and any acknowledged suffix beyond the recoverable cut. |
| 3 | **Set a defensible performance envelope for each mode.** Use the actual client retry policy, realistic hot and cold accounts, cold connections, checkpoints, compaction, and learner catch-up. | Report offered, submitted, posted, rejected, retried, dropped and completed counts with latency percentiles, archive lag, catch-up duration and peak disk use. Retain the workload and run transcripts. |
| 4 | **Make integration repeatable.** Ship a runnable first payment and same-ID retry, a reservation-balance lookup, and separate retryable capacity errors from fixed input limits. | A new backend team can create accounts, move funds, recover a lost reply and reconcile a partial capture using the public interface. Add caller attribution and roles when multiple trust groups use the ledger. |
| 5 | **Keep structural changes small.** Consolidate service-mode policy, isolate optional Kafka build dependencies, and retain replay fixtures and failure tests across releases. | A core change is testable without networking. A voter-only build needs no Kafka tooling. Replaying an older history after an upgrade yields the same decisions, funding allocations and entry IDs. |

The architecture supports further integration. A production decision depends on meeting agreed targets in the intended deployment. Cross-host behavior, multi-broker failover, long-running storage growth and the 1 TB recovery case remain outside the local benchmarks.
