# Rust Raft ledger release qualification

**9 November 2026. The candidate is ready for local integration against the tested contracts. Production rollout is a no-go in both modes pending deployment qualification.** The owner requested local drills and left the launch mode and targets undecided. Every voter, broker and simulated independent volume used the same Apple M2 with 8 GiB RAM. Process isolation and mTLS traffic here exercise the protocols. Host loss and power-loss durability require independent hardware. [Owner's qualification checklist](production-readiness-goal.md) · [Environment and tools](measurements/rc-local/environment.log)

| Intended use | Decision | Condition to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backend integration or local staging | Go | Use the published client contract, preserve original IDs and payloads, and choose the acknowledgement mode explicitly. [Integration guide](backend-integration.md) |
| Synchronous journal, three production voters | No-go | Agree on peak and sustained offered work, operation mix, p99, time to first payment and time to three healthy disks. Place the journal in its own failure domain. On the intended mounts check locking, atomic rename and crash durability, then reconcile every acknowledged acceptance **and rejection** after loss of all voters. Protected results must pause through a journal outage. |
| Three voters and permanent non-voting learner | No-go | In addition to workload and time targets, select an acceptable acknowledged-loss window and archive lag alert/response. Cut the learner off during writes, destroy all four disks, fence after isolating the old majority and reconcile independent receipts to the restored prefix. Test the actual broker, storage and consumer deduplication settings. Kafka-only service has no independent ledger recovery copy. |
| Long-retention service or 1 TB per voter | No-go | Provision space for retained history, pins, live disks, staging and backups. Run the actual retention/compaction schedule and a full cold 1 TB replacement over the intended link during writes. Measure peak allocated space, time to first payment and all three disks and check the restored receipts. |

### Return to service after total disk loss

Both timers start when the drill quiesces the old group. Each uses a small local ledger, new disk directories and a new generation. The fourth replacement in learner mode was a non-voting member. Every replacement disk was checked against the externally saved decisions.

| Release-build drill | Receipts matched on each disk | First new payment | Three voters applied the new payment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Protected journal, three fresh voters | 9 of 9 | 1.731 s | 1.732 s, through index 16 |
| Learner archive, three fresh voters and a fresh learner | 4 of 6 | 1.937 s | 1.943 s, through index 8 |

The learner drill deliberately withheld archive coverage of an acceptance and a rejection. Both had been acknowledged and delivered to Kafka, and both were missing from the restored ledger. The new learner subsequently archived the post-recovery payment. The two missing receipts demonstrate the outcome of this particular outage. Production loss and recovery-time bounds require deployment tests. ### Abrupt process loss

In the packaged protected cluster, five acknowledgements were saved outside the voter disks. With journal confirmation held at index 6, the test submitted three more requests and observed the leader apply them through index 9. All three voters were killed while those requests were unanswered. Following election on the original disks, the client looked up all three decisions before retrying. The five saved receipts and all eight same-ID retries matched. The fresh payment was entry 5, entry 6 was absent, and the verified wallet balance was 81. The client saw a transport-level `UNKNOWN` for interrupted writes. Both packaged node variants passed. [Standard binary](measurements/rc-local/protected-process-crash.log) · [Kafka-free binary](measurements/rc-local/protected-voter-process-crash.log)

In learner mode the fixture killed both live voters and the learner while the third voter was offline, removed all four old disk directories and restored four replacements. Every replacement matched the four covered receipts and lacked the same one acceptance and one rejection that had reached Kafka beyond the archive cut. A fresh payment succeeded at 1.402 s and all three voters had applied it by 1.403 s. The external archive, Kafka and receipt store remained available. [Learner process-crash transcript](measurements/rc-local/learner-process-crash.log)

The standalone receipt checker used the authenticated public API to compare an external inventory with the restored learner-mode ledger. It identified the missing acceptance and rejection by ID, passed the covered inventory, and separately reported a mismatched posting, invalid evidence and an RPC refusal for the wrong ledger. It issues read requests only. The mount probe also exercised real journal locking, publication and replay using two local processes. Both utilities are ready to run on an isolated deployment. ## Qualifying the intended deployment

1. The owner records the launch mode, normal and peak arrival rates, operation mix, p99 limit, permissible acknowledged loss, and the two recovery deadlines before the first trial. Include the intended journal, broker and backup durability settings, deployed binary hashes and mount identities with the results. Record the measurement interval and which arrival-to-reply requests count toward the latency target.
2. Drive the isolated staging ledger from a client outside the voter failure domains. Save the request ID and complete ordered payload before sending. Persist each returned decision on the client's independent storage and retain its acknowledgement time. During normal and peak traffic, include rejects, same-ID retries, quorum reads, checkpointing and voter catch-up. Count offers, sender drops, unresolved writes and terminal replies independently so an apparently good p99 cannot hide work that never reached the ledger.
3. On the intended independent journal service, exercise separate mount clients and the storage provider's actual failure and restart procedure. Use a disposable ledger and the intended durability settings. After recovery, read from a freshly mounted client, scrub the recorded prefix, then restore and compare the external receipts. A protected acceptance or rejection acknowledged before the failure must resolve to its original decision. Resolve calls interrupted before receipt by their original ID and payload.
4. For learner mode, cut archive connectivity while quorum writes continue. Record the last independently verified archive index, the acknowledged requests after it, and the separate Kafka cursor. After isolating the old majority and restoring fresh disks, reconcile covered receipts, missing accepts, missing rejects and Kafka-only effects. Compare the acknowledgement times of the missing receipts with the owner's loss window. Exercise the actual downstream consumer with a replay and verify that its external effect occurs once.
5. Run the interruption timer on the external client. Record elapsed time to its first successful fresh payment and then to three distinct voters reporting the replacement generation and an applied index covering that payment. Compare both against the agreed deadlines. For a 1 TB claim, repeat from a physically populated dataset at that size with the intended retention, link and backing storage. Record allocated bytes, including staging, pinned history and concurrent writes, through recovery. Use the emitted network test executable paths. Each trial creates fresh data, ports and certificates. The SDK comparison alternates builds and offers 300 new payment requests/s for ten seconds, with at most 64 concurrent calls, an intended same-ID replay on every eighth offer, a hot account and a rotating set of wallets sharing a 2 KiB decoded-account cache. One eighth of new commands are intended to hit a balance floor. The latency percentiles measure scheduled arrival through the first accepted or rejected decision, including admission retries. The open-loop sender counts offers skipped at its concurrency limit. Replays and overload resubmissions count explicit SDK invocations. Internal seed-discovery attempts are outside those counters. Balances, entry uniqueness and final journal coverage are checked after each phase.

The separate public mTLS workload uses eight warmed connections, 32 callers, a mix of reads, original payments and same-ID retries, a hot wallet, compaction/reclamation and a returning follower. Its wire attempt counts include each explicit retry. The startup and first call of the SDK example exercise cold connections separately. The sustained local workload samples allocated disk blocks across the voters, learner when present and journal; hard-linked files count once per inode. Samples can miss short peaks. Archive lag observations on the two independently read indexes can straddle a commit. Each local run uses a shared physical disk. Background compilation and other workstation activity can affect individual results.

The SDK harness defaults to `LEDGER_QUAL_OBSERVER=active`. Each periodic sample confirms the independent journal head under its writer lock, syncs the directory and walks the voter and archive files. To measure the cost of that observer, run the same prebuilt test with `LEDGER_QUAL_OBSERVER=passive`. This samples the protected leader's confirmed index or the learner archive worker's locally verified frontier. A missing verification is reported as `NA`. Both modes measure disk allocation at the beginning and end and require the independent journal to cover the final applied index. Passive mode omits periodic disk scans and reports `NA` for peak allocation. Each run prints observer calls and sample durations. A direct invocation is `LEDGER_QUAL_OBSERVER=passive LEDGER_QUAL_MODE=protected /path/to/network-test api::capacity::sdk_capacity_and_storage --exact --ignored --nocapture`.
